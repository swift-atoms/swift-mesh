# swift-mesh

Use this package when you need a surface as data: a list of vertices and a list of faces that name them by index, over a vertex type you choose. Start from `Mesh<Vertex>`. Build one from your own vertices and faces, from a grid with `Mesh(columns:rows:seams:vertex:)`, which is how every parametric surface becomes a mesh, or from the ready surfaces `torus`, `sphere` and a `Bezier<Vertex>.Patch` from swift-bezier. When the vertices are `Point<3, Scalar>` from swift-point, `areas()` and `normals(per:)` give the face and vertex normals. A face is a `Polygon<Index<Vertex>>` from swift-polygon, so triangles, quads and larger faces live side by side until you ask for `triangulated`. It holds no materials, texture coordinates or transforms: put what a vertex carries in the vertex type and use `map`. It ships no models: a model such as the Utah teapot is a consumer's patch data, tessellated patch by patch, joined with `appending` and `welded`.

## Laws

- A face is a closed cycle of at least three vertex indices, typed `Index<Vertex>` so that a vertex position cannot be mistaken for a count or for a position in another list. Construction fails with `.indexOutOfBounds` when a face names an index outside `vertices`; decoding enforces the same. Nothing else is checked: repeated indices, unused vertices, faces sharing an edge in the same direction and holes are all accepted.
- Equality and hashing compare the vertices in order and the faces as given. Two meshes that list the same surface differently are different values.
- `map` transforms the vertices in order and keeps the faces. It stops at the first failure.
- `reversed` reverses every face, keeping each face's first index, and `reversed.reversed == self`.
- `triangulated` splits each face of n indices into n − 2 triangles that fan from its first index, in face order, with the same orientation. Triangles are left as they are, so it is idempotent.
- `appending(_:)` lists this mesh's vertices and faces and then the other's, with the other's indices shifted by this mesh's vertex count. `empty` is neutral on both sides.
- `welded` merges vertices that are equal into the first of them, keeps the order of first occurrences, and renames the indices in the faces. No two vertices of the result are equal, every face still resolves to the same vertices, the face count is unchanged, and welding twice changes nothing.
- `polygon(at:)` is the face with each index replaced by its vertex. It traps for an index outside `faces`.
- A grid of `columns` × `rows` cells has one quad per cell, listed row by row. An open direction has one more line of vertices than cells; a closed direction has as many, and its last line of cells joins the first line of vertices. Vertices are requested and stored row by row, at index `row × lines per row + column`. The quad of cell (c, r) is (c, r), (c + 1, r), (c + 1, r + 1), (c, r + 1): counterclockwise when the column direction is drawn to the right and the row direction upward. It traps unless there is at least one column and one row.
- `areas()` gives each face's vector area by Newell's method, measured from the face's first vertex so that the result does not depend on where the mesh sits: its length is the area of the face's projection onto its best plane, and it points to the side from which the face is seen counterclockwise. `normals(per: .face)` is that vector at unit length. `normals(per: .vertex)` is the unit vector of the sum of the vector areas of the faces that name the vertex, so larger faces weigh more. A zero or non-finite sum gives the zero vector, as for a degenerate face or an unused vertex. Reversing a mesh negates every normal.
- `torus(major:minor:segments:sides:)` is the grid closed in both directions on the torus around the z axis whose tube centre lies on the circle of radius `major` in the xy plane, with tube radius `minor`. It has `segments × sides` vertices and as many quads, is closed (every edge is used once in each direction), has Euler characteristic 0, and faces outward. Its first vertex is (`major + minor`, 0, 0).
- `sphere(radius:meridians:parallels:)` is centred on the origin with its poles on the z axis. Each pole is one vertex, so it has `meridians × (parallels − 1) + 2` vertices; the bands next to the poles are triangles and the rest quads, `meridians × parallels` faces in all. It is closed, has Euler characteristic 2, and faces outward. It traps for fewer than three meridians or two parallels.
- Torus and sphere vertices are computed with the scalar's own `sin` and `cos`, so they are as repeatable across platforms as those functions are.
- `Mesh(patch, columns:rows:interpolating:)` is the open grid whose vertex at (column, row) is `patch.value(at: column / columns, row / rows, interpolating:)`. Its corner vertices are the patch's corner control points and its boundary vertices lie on the patch's boundary curves, exactly, when the interpolation returns its endpoints exactly at 0 and 1. Patches that share a boundary curve in the same direction therefore produce equal vertices along it, which `welded` joins.
- A mesh encodes as `vertices` and `faces`, each face a bare array of indices.
